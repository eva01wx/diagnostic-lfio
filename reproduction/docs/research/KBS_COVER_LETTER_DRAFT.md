Dear Editors of *Knowledge-Based Systems*,

Please consider “Compact Rule Distillation with Diagnostic Allocation and Certified Feature Forgetting”
for publication as a research article. The authors are Xu Wang and Zhiqiang Chen,
in that order. I am the corresponding author of this manuscript.

The article addresses the decision that follows a poor compact surrogate:
whether to improve its search, enrich its numerical predicates, or change its
description allowance. It combines an exact empirical decoder floor with
finite-pool integer-programming bounds on the same real fitting samples,
separating capacity and search intervals under explicit condition budgets.
A fitting-only controller makes corresponding intervention probes under an
absolute allowance of scored-neighbor evaluations.

A prospectively frozen extension uses six previously unused datasets, two
native numerical teacher families, three splits and clean/noisy supervision.
Its 72 settings yield 1,728 budget selections across eight methods, including
official FastSRS and continuous-input NeuRules. The primary controller-minus-
median difference is +1.19 percentage points, with a dataset-cluster interval
of [-0.46, +2.95]. Fixed MDL enrichment performs better than this conservative
controller. The article therefore claims an executable attribution and
allocation study, rather than a generally superior rule optimizer. Real-task
solver bounds, incomplete feasibility and the single-class-teacher sensitivity
are reported alongside accuracy, actual model size and computational cost.

A separate explicit-rule-generalization extension uses new splits of the same
six datasets and 648 base/budget fits. Numerical interval proofs validate
equivalent simplification; a fixed grow-only expansion path is selected using
separate calibration and simultaneous binomial harm bounds. The bounded
variant reduces conditions by 4.78% on average with a test macro-fidelity
change of -0.017 percentage points. The sampling assumptions and classical
origins of the operators and confidence limits are explicit; these structural
savings do not establish human comprehension.

A subsequent feature-forgetting module compiles necessary and sufficient
conditions over retained numerical coordinates and certifies partial-input
predictions or abstains. It reuses the known source programs and accounts for
representation growth and resource-limited conservative failures. Logical
forgetting and the quantifier duality are explicitly attributed to earlier
work. The extension adds exact rational background difference constraints, an
explicit infeasible-input state, cached residual queries and coverage-constrained
cost selection. On the same 648 Original programs, the cost policy increases
hidden controlled feature cost by 1.97 percentage points over coverage-first
selection (descriptive six-dataset interval [0.93, 3.10]), at 89.9% test certified
coverage. The constraints are tested on known synthetic domains; feature costs
are controlled scenarios. Neither a new forgetting theory nor minimum human
explanations are claimed.

The current source, code and experiment-record packages contain fixed
protocols, baseline provenance, data hashes, exports, decisions, predictions
and regeneration scripts. The release is identified as
`contextual-forgetting-2026-10-16` under
`docs/research/contextual_forgetting_2026-10-16/`; its package index is
`docs/research/CURRENT_RELEASE.md`. All 216 controller decisions have been replayed.
Earlier development cohorts are presented separately and are not pooled to
increase the independent study's effective dataset count. The manuscript
discloses AI-assisted preparation and its scientific limitations.
